Current workflow · 1 of 4

The justification review end to end

There are four stages on the official map. The fourth is drawn dashed and isn't connected to the others.

Phase 1
Primary Review

The manager or delegate reviews access in EERS. Approves or revokes, and writes a justification if the access is high-risk and low-commonality.

Phase 2
Challenge & Escalation

MSaaS sends a weekly sample. ERQA evaluates, challenges, escalates, then closes each case as PASS or FAIL.

Phase 3
HR Reporting

Fails go to remedial training (monthly LMS report) and/or HR (weekly, Accountability Framework).

Phase 4 ?
IAM & Deferred Revocations

No detail in the material I have. In scope or not?

Who's involved

  • EERS Reviewer: a manager or delegate, writes the justification
  • Reviewer's manager: CC'd on the challenge, receives the escalation
  • MSaaS team: produces the weekly sample
  • ERQA analysts: evaluate, challenge, decide
  • HR: runs the predefined HR process

When a justification is needed

  • The access is high-risk, based on what it lets the person do and EERS attribute codes (not the IS risk rating)
  • and it's low-commonality: fewer than 25% of the reviewer's direct reports have it
  • Examples: privileged, external, money transfer
  • Applies to semi-annual, ad hoc and transfer reviews

What managers must answer

  • Reason the access is needed (dropdown + context)
  • Frequency of use (dropdown)
  • Worker's role
  • Business process
  • Reason for low commonality

The dataset has all 5 as columns: Template Name (the reason dropdown), FREQUENCY, JUST_ROLE, JUST_PROCESS, JUST_COMM.

Phase 2: the challenge and escalation loop

Two 7-calendar-day clocks, several decision points, and an email at every step.

#StepOutcome
1ERQA evaluates the sampled justification against the defined criteriaMeets them → PASS · Doesn't → challenge
2Challenge email to the reviewer (their manager on CC)⏱ 7 calendar days
3Did the reviewer act?No → escalate (step 5)
4Acted: was the access revoked? If not, did the revised justification pass?Revoked → FAIL ✅ · Passed → PASS · Still fails → escalate
5Escalation email to the reviewer's manager⏱ 7 more calendar days
6Acted after escalation, and did it pass?Yes → PASS · No action, still fails, or revoked → FAIL
7FAIL → Phase 3 HR Reporting
✅ Confirmed: revoking = FAIL, at either stage. If the reviewer revokes the access after the challenge or after the escalation, the case is a FAIL. Revoking means they accept the original justification was wrong. The process map doesn't draw the after-escalation branch, so it may be worth adding in the next version.

Why this loop is costly (my hypothesis)

  • Every challenge means writing and sending an email (in batches, by hand), tracking a deadline, rechecking EERS and matching replies in the mailbox
  • An escalation repeats all of that
  • The deadlines are fixed rules (+7 days), so a system could track them

Key discoveries

Six things that stood out

Each one is a discovery to validate, not a conclusion.

D1

A challenge fixes most justifications

2,446 cases were challenged and only 417 ended as fails. Most managers correct the justification once they're challenged. The cost is probably the chasing (emails, clocks, rechecks), not the decision to challenge.

D2

The criteria are partly mechanical

The official reasons for a challenge are: text copied across fields, vague answers like "BAU", role and process mixed up, no low-commonality reason, acronyms not spelled out. Several of these could be flagged automatically for the analyst.

D3

The deadlines and routing follow fixed rules

+7 days, +7 days, no-action vs. acted, 3 fails in 12 months, weekly vs. monthly. A system can apply rules like these the same way every time and log them for audit.

D4

The training/HR numbers don't add up

If every fail followed the Phase 3 rules, 358 training + 50 HR would need a negative number of no-action fails. Either the columns are incomplete, the rules changed over time, or I've misread the fields.

D5 ✅

The "template" is a dropdown

"Access is required for their job function" (about 96% of rows) is the Template Name dropdown, i.e., the reason the access is needed. The column headers confirm it. The useful signal is in the free-text columns JUST_ROLE, JUST_PROCESS and JUST_COMM.

D6

There's already an internal AI tool

The guide points managers to the Stylus /justification-review preset to draft and check justifications. Could ERQA use something similar on the assessment side?

One point on the trend: the fail rate halved from 13.0% to 6.7%. To show that's real improvement and not lighter QA, I'd show it next to the sample size and the challenge rate.
Proposal

Six modules, mapped to the workflow

They can be built and adopted one at a time. In every module, the analyst keeps the final say.

M1 · Intake Pipeline

Pick up the weekly .txt automatically, remove duplicates, add the extra fields, load into the case tracker, and log row counts.
Replaces: the manual load into MS Access, the queries and the upload.

M2 · Case Tracker & SLA Engine

One record per case with its status, stage, and the 7-day and escalation due dates computed automatically. Views for "due soon" and "overdue".
Replaces: tracking deadlines by hand and rechecking status.

M3 · Justification Pre-Screen

Flags matching the C1–C5 criteria, with the reason shown for each. A suggestion only; the analyst decides.
Supports: faster, more consistent assessment.

M4 · Communication Automation

Challenge and escalation emails drafted from templates with the case details and the specific missing criteria. The analyst approves before anything is sent. Replies matched to cases by case ID.
Replaces: writing emails in batches by hand.

M5 · Outcome Routing & Reporting

Classify each fail as no-action or acted, check 3 fails in 12 months automatically, and produce the weekly HR and monthly LMS reports with a routing log.
Replaces: the manual Phase 3 work.

M6 · Governance Dashboard

Pass/fail/exclusion trend, challenge rate, backlog and SLA breaches, and hotspots by app, entitlement, segment and reviewer. Country values standardized.
Supports: leadership reporting and audit.

OPTIONAL M7 AI assist, only if an internal tool (e.g., Stylus) is approved for this data: summarize revised justifications and draft wording. It is never a decision-maker.

Solution options

The same modules on different platforms, depending on what we're allowed to use

TIER A · Lowest friction

Excel / Power Query / Power BI

  • Power Query replaces the MS Access load and queries (M1)
  • Formula-based deadlines and flags (M2, M3)
  • Power BI dashboard (M6)

Few approvals needed · refreshed by hand · limited automation

TIER B · Recommended to explore

SharePoint + Power Automate (+ Power Apps)

  • SharePoint List as the case tracker, with version history for audit
  • Power Automate for deadlines, reminders, email drafts and weekly/monthly reports
  • Power Apps for the analyst workbench

Builds on the existing SharePoint List · needs Power Platform approval

TIER C · Most capable

Internal app + database

  • Scheduled pipeline and an internal database
  • A web workbench like the prototype
  • Optional internal AI assist

The most approvals and engineering effort · possible model risk review

Design principles, whatever the tier

Human decides. The system suggests, drafts and reminders; analysts approve.
Explainable. Rules before models, and every flag shows why.
Data stays inside. No external services without approval.
Audit by default. Every status change, email and override is logged.

Volumes, time and intake

Click a question to see why I'm asking. ★ = must ask today.

★ How many cases come in each weekly sample, and how many are challenged and escalated per week? Are there peak periods?
Why: the dataset gives yearly totals, not the weekly load. Weekly volume determines how much time automation can save and how much load the system has to handle. Peaks (e.g., semi-annual campaigns) show when the pain is worst.
★ Roughly how many minutes per case for: the first assessment, writing a challenge email, rechecking EERS, and closing a case with evidence?
Why: this is the baseline. Without it I can't prove impact, and I can't tell which module to build first. The aim is to put the biggest time cost first.
★ Which step do you find most tedious or error-prone?
Why: the team's own pain points matter more than my guesses. Fixing what annoys people most gets the tool adopted, and you know things the diagrams don't show.
What do the MS Access queries do: which fields identify a duplicate, and which fields are added and from where?
Why: M1 has to reproduce this logic exactly. If I miss a hidden step, the data going into the tracker would be wrong.
Is the .txt from MSaaS always the same format? Who owns it, and could it be delivered differently (e.g., straight to SharePoint)?
Why: automation depends on a stable input. Knowing the owner tells me who to coordinate with on changes, and whether we can skip the file drop entirely.
Who uses the SharePoint List today, and what views and columns do you rely on?
Why: if Tier B is chosen, the existing list becomes the case tracker. I'd rather extend what you use than replace it.

Assessment, challenge and outcome rules

Why: the pre-screen must follow the official criteria and nothing beyond them. That's how it stays defensible in an audit.

Why: this tells me whether the tool needs an approval step, and whether consistency between analysts is something leadership cares about.
How do you track the 7-day deadlines today, and how often are they missed?
Why: this shows how much M2 is worth. If deadlines are tracked from memory or in a spreadsheet, missed escalations are a control risk as well as a time cost.
Are challenge and escalation emails templated? How big is a batch, and how do you match replies to cases?
Why: M4 drafts emails from templates. Matching replies to cases is a hidden manual cost that a case ID in the subject line could remove. I also need to know if sending from the shared mailbox can be automated.

Why: this is a lookup across history that's easy to get wrong by hand. I need the exact report formats to generate them automatically (M5).
